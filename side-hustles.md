# Zero-Investment Online Side Hustles: What Actually Pays

*Prepared October 2026 (you asked about 2025; the figures below are 2025–2026 data). Pay rates come from cited sources. **All 30/60/90-day earnings are my estimates**, assuming ~10 hours/week, starting from zero, with no existing audience or portfolio.*

## The honest summary

Almost every true zero-investment hustle is **selling your time or skill**. The "passive" ones (Etsy templates, affiliate sites, YouTube) cost almost nothing to start, but they take 6–12 months to pay meaningfully, so they lose on a 90-day horizon. The highest 90-day earners are the ones where you **find your own clients** (appointment setting, websites). Platforms that hand you work (AI training, user testing) pay faster but cap out lower.

**Earnings below are what you'd make *during* month 1, month 2 and month 3, not cumulative totals. All are estimates.**

**What I left out, and why:** Dropshipping and reselling need inventory or ad money. Affiliate marketing and YouTube usually take 6+ months to earn. Survey apps pay a few dollars an hour. "Side hustle" courses, MLMs and day-trading aren't zero-risk, and most are traps.

**Taxes:** All of these are 1099/self-employment income. Set aside roughly 25–30% (self-employment tax is 15.3%, plus income tax; the exact amount depends on your situation).

## 1. Commission-based B2B appointment setting

- **Business model:** You cold-call and email prospects for a company and book sales meetings for their closers. You're paid per meeting, or a percentage of closed deals. Zero cost: the company provides the leads and tools.
- **Who pays:** B2B companies (agencies, SaaS, home-service firms, coaches) that need pipeline without hiring a full-time SDR. Outsourced agencies charge them **$550–$1,700 per qualified B2B appointment**, and commission-only setters typically earn **$15–$100 per appointment** ([Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/appointment-setters)). Real postings include **$100 per booked-and-attended call plus 10% of closed deals** ([Salesfolks posting](https://salesfolks.com/job/appointment-setter-remote-1099-commission-only)) and **15% of every deal your meetings close** ([Remotive posting](https://remotive.com/remote/jobs/sales/setter-6034303)).
- **Realistic 30/60/90 (est.):** Month 1 $0–300 (onboarding, learning the offer, the first meetings). Month 2 $300–1,500. Month 3 $600–3,000. Assumes ~10 hrs/week at roughly 2–8 attended meetings/week by month 3 at $50–100 each. Commission-only means **months with $0 are possible.**
- **Watch out:** Many commission-only offers are bad: weak products, no leads, or they pay only on *closed* deals that you can't control. Prefer pay per **attended** meeting, and ask what current setters actually earn.
- **First 3 steps today:**
  1. Write a 3-line pitch about yourself: "Sales development intern and team lead; [X] calls/day; booked [Y] meetings." Put it in your LinkedIn headline.
  2. Apply to 10 commission-based setter roles (Remotive, Salesfolks, LinkedIn Jobs search "appointment setter commission"). Skip any that pay *only* on closed deals.
  3. Or skip job boards: DM 15 founders of small B2B agencies on LinkedIn offering to book meetings for $75–100 per attended meeting, with no retainer. This is the higher-paying route.

## 2. AI-built websites and automations for local businesses

- **Business model:** Use AI coding tools and free hosting tiers to build simple sites, booking pages, or intake-form automations for local businesses. Charge a project fee plus optional monthly maintenance. Zero cost: you use free tiers, and the client pays for their own domain and hosting.
- **Who pays:** Small local businesses (contractors, salons, clinics, restaurants). Freelancer-built small-business sites typically run **$1,500–$5,000**; budget-platform freelancers charge **$299–$499**, and mid-level freelancers **$1,999–$2,999** ([Seahawk Media](https://seahawkmedia.com/tech/small-business-website-costs/), [WebsiteSetup](https://websitesetup.org/website-cost/)).
- **Realistic 30/60/90 (est.):** Month 1 $0–750 (one discounted portfolio site). Month 2 $500–2,000 (1–2 sites at $500–$1,000). Month 3 $1,000–3,000, plus $50–$100/mo maintenance retainers per client (est.).
- **Watch out:** "Website" is crowded, and you're competing with Wix/Squarespace DIY. Sell an *outcome* ("more booked appointments," "online quotes") to a niche, and avoid endless revisions with a fixed scope in writing.
- **First 3 steps today:**
  1. Pick one niche, e.g., Utah County landscapers or HVAC shops. Find 20 on Google Maps with no website or a bad one.
  2. Build a free demo homepage for the best prospect using their real name, services and reviews (2–3 hours with AI tools).
  3. Call or walk in and show it: "I built this for you. It's yours for $500 including setup; $50/mo if you want me to maintain it."

## 3. AI training / data annotation

- **Business model:** AI companies pay contractors to write prompts, grade model answers and check reasoning. You're paid hourly or per task. Zero cost; it requires passing an assessment.
- **Who pays:** AI labs through platforms like DataAnnotation and Outlier. DataAnnotation's reported rates are about **$20–$30/hr for general work and $40–$50+/hr for coding** ([Second Talent](https://www.secondtalent.com/alternatives/dataannotation/), [TalentsForAI](https://talentsforai.com/blog/is-dataannotation-tech-legit/)). Outlier is reported at roughly **$15–$30/hr**, more for specialists ([Breaking Even](https://breakingeven.online/blog/outlier-ai-pay-per-task-2026)). Neither publishes official rates up front.
- **Realistic 30/60/90 (est.):** Month 1 $0–400 (acceptance can take days to weeks, and many applicants never hear back). Months 2–3 $300–800 each at ~10 hrs/week, *if work is available*.
- **Watch out:** The main complaint is an **inconsistent task supply**; Outlier was downgraded in one 2026 review for empty queues ([Breaking Even tier list](https://breakingeven.online/blog/ai-training-jobs-2026-platform-tier-list)). Apply to several platforms. Never pay a fee to "get into" one; that's a scam signal.
- **First 3 steps today:**
  1. Apply to DataAnnotation and Outlier, plus 1–2 others from the same lists.
  2. Take the assessments when you're fresh and unhurried: write carefully, they grade quality.
  3. If you can code, take the coding assessment too; it roughly doubles the rate.

## 4. Niche freelance writing (finance/legal)

- **Business model:** Write blog posts, explainers, and newsletters for businesses. You're paid per word or per piece.
- **Who pays:** B2B companies, agencies, and financial and legal firms. The Editorial Freelancers Association's 2026 chart puts blog posts at **$0.25–$0.40/word** for professionals; direct B2B clients pay **$0.25–$0.75/word**, and content agencies **$0.10–$0.25/word**. In one survey, nearly half of writers charge only $0.05–$0.10/word ([BestWriting](https://bestwriting.com/content-writing-rates), [Ruul](https://ruul.io/blog/freelance-writer-rates)).
- **Realistic 30/60/90 (est.):** Month 1 $0–300. Month 2 $150–600 (2–4 posts at ~$150). Month 3 $300–1,200.
- **Watch out:** AI has crushed generic blog-writing demand. Only *niche expertise* (personal finance, insurance, legal topics for law-firm marketers) still sells. Don't write legal *advice*.
- **First 3 steps today:**
  1. Write 2 strong samples in your niche (e.g., "How workers' comp experience mods work," "SBA loans for first-time buyers") and publish them on LinkedIn or Medium.
  2. Make a one-page portfolio (free Google Doc or Notion).
  3. Pitch 10 finance and insurance content agencies or marketing managers with the samples and a $0.10–0.15/word intro rate.

## 5. Online tutoring

- **Business model:** One-on-one sessions over video, for a subject or test prep.
- **Who pays:** Students and parents. **Wyzant lets you set your rate and takes 25%** (dropping to 20% after $2,000 earned). College-level help runs $50–$90/hr. **Varsity Tutors sets pay at about $15–$40/hr**, with many tutors reporting $15–$20 ([SideHusl](https://sidehusl.com/12-best-tutoring-platforms/), [SideHusl on Varsity](https://sidehusl.com/varsity-tutors/)).
- **Realistic 30/60/90 (est.):** Month 1 $0–200 (no reviews yet). Month 2 $200–600. Month 3 $400–1,000 (at $40/hr gross, ~$30 net after commission, ~8–10 sessions/week by month 3 at best).
- **Watch out:** Getting first students without reviews is slow. Start below market, then raise rates.
- **First 3 steps today:**
  1. Make a Wyzant profile for subjects you're strong in (accounting, finance, Excel, intro econ, SAT/ACT math).
  2. Price low at first (~$30–35/hr) to land the first 5 reviews.
  3. Post in your university's tutoring/Facebook/Discord groups: private students skip the platform cut.

## 6. UGC videos for brands

- **Business model:** Film short product videos on your phone that brands run as ads. You don't need followers; brands pay for the content.
- **Who pays:** DTC/e-commerce brands. Beginners typically get **$50–$150 per video**; the average is about $175–$200, and usage rights are charged extra ([Billo](https://billo.app/blog/ugc-rates/), [Insense](https://insense.pro/blog/ugc-creator-rates)).
- **Realistic 30/60/90 (est.):** Month 1 $0–150. Month 2 $100–600. Month 3 $300–1,200.
- **Watch out:** Very crowded, and some brands want free content "for exposure." Your portfolio matters more than anything.
- **First 3 steps today:**
  1. Film 3 sample videos with products you already own: a hook, a demo, and an unboxing.
  2. Post them as a portfolio (free Canva page or a TikTok/IG account just for UGC).
  3. Sign up for UGC marketplaces (Billo, Insense) and pitch 10 small brands by email.

## 7. Paid user testing

- **Business model:** Record yourself using websites and apps while thinking aloud.
- **Who pays:** Product teams through UserTesting and similar platforms. About **$10 per test**, with a $3–$60 range; testers report **$10–$300/week**, and the work is irregular ([SoftwareTestingHelp](https://www.softwaretestinghelp.com/usertesting-review/), [Finder](https://finder.com/make-money-online/how-to-earn-money-user-testing)).
- **Realistic 30/60/90 (est.):** $20–150, then $40–200, then $40–200. The ceiling is low because you can't control how many tests you get.
- **First 3 steps today:**
  1. Apply to UserTesting and pass the practice test.
  2. Join 2–3 others listed in the same guides to increase test volume.
  3. Turn on notifications; tests go fast to whoever grabs them first.

## 8. Digital templates on Etsy

- **Business model:** Sell downloadable templates (budget spreadsheets, Notion planners, resume templates).
- **Who pays:** Etsy shoppers. Costs: **$0.20 per listing** (so technically almost zero), a **6.5% transaction fee**, and payment processing of about 3% + $0.25 ([Marmalead](https://blog.marmalead.com/etsy-fees-explained-2025/), [Checkout Page](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/etsy-fees)).
- **Realistic 30/60/90 (est.):** $0–50, $0–150, $20–400. Results are extremely uneven; most new shops sell little for months.
- **Watch out:** Template marketplaces are saturated with AI-made products. Only very specific niches stand out.
- **First 3 steps today:**
  1. Search Etsy for a niche you know (e.g., "college student budget spreadsheet," "law school outline template") and note what sells and the gaps in the reviews.
  2. Make 3 better versions in Google Sheets/Notion.
  3. List all 3 (~$0.60 total) with keyword-rich titles and real screenshots.

## What I'd do in your position

1. **This week:** Start #1 (appointment setting) and #2 (websites). Both use skills you already have, and both pay the most within 90 days. Apply to AI training (#3) the same day as a backup; it takes 30 minutes and can pay while the other two ramp up.
2. **Skip** user testing and Etsy unless you just want pocket money.
3. **Link it to your bigger plan:** Booking meetings for other companies is practice and proof for the group business in `ai-service-ideas.md` (the Contract-Winner Lead Feed upsell is literally booked meetings), and website clients are future buyers for it.
