## 3. Deep dives

### #1: Contract-Winner Lead Feed (score 27)

**Why it works:** When a company wins a public contract, it suddenly needs bonding, insurance, equipment, staff, and often accounting help (DCAA-style cost accounting). That's a buying trigger. Vendors who sell to these companies would love to know *who just won*, *how big*, and *who to call*, but they won't learn USASpending or pay $13k+/yr for GovWin. You deliver the list.

**Exact offer**
- **Tier 1, "Feed": $400–$600/mo.** On the 1st and 15th of each month, a Google Sheet/CSV with every new award in the buyer's chosen geography and NAICS codes above a size threshold. Each row has company, award amount, agency, scope summary, decision-maker name, email and phone, plus a one-line "why now" angle. Delivered within 3 business days of each cycle.
- **Exclusivity add-on: +$200/mo.** Only one buyer per category per region (e.g., one surety agency for Utah). This creates urgency and raises retention.
- **Tier 2, "Meetings": $1,500–$3,000/mo, or a base plus ~$150–$250 per qualified meeting.** You run the outbound to those winners and book meetings. This is your SDR experience, sold as an outcome.

**Target customer (start narrow)**
- **First buyer:** Utah/Mountain West **surety bond producers and commercial insurance agencies** writing contractors (construction, landscaping, janitorial, IT services). Public construction contracts often require bonds, so a newly awarded contractor is a hot bonding prospect. Your workers' comp knowledge makes you credible here.
- **Second buyers:** equipment rental yards, construction staffing agencies, CPA firms that serve government contractors.

**Pricing evidence**
- Done-for-you verified lead lists: about €290 for 300 leads one-off, or €590/mo for up to 800 leads ([Hamster Digital](https://hamsterdigital.it.com/?p=29)).
- Raw data is cheap: HigherGov starts at about $500/yr; GovWin averages about $29k/yr ([HigherGov](https://www.highergov.com/alt/govwin), [Prospeo on GovWin](https://prospeo.io/s/deltek-govwin-iq-pricing-reviews-pros-and-cons)). **Implication:** Price to the buyer's sale value, not to the data cost. One bonded contractor or one workers' comp policy is worth far more than $500 to an agent (est.).
- Outsourced appointment setting: $150–$600 per qualified meeting, $3k–$8k/mo retainers, and hybrid "base + per meeting" is the most common structure ([Funnl](https://funnl.ai/inside-sales-outsourcing-scale-from-5-to-25-meetings-per-month-without-hiring-a-single-sdr/), [Revnew](https://revnew.com/blog/sdr-outsourcing-cost), [Activated Scale](https://www.activatedscale.com/feeds/blog/outsourced-sales-team-cost-month)).

**Behind-the-scenes workflow**
1. **Pull awards (automated):** USASpending.gov API for federal awards by recipient state/place of performance; SAM.gov for new notices; Utah's state procurement portal and county/city award postings for SLED (state and local). Your existing gov-contract platform code likely already handles the federal pulls.
2. **Filter and dedupe (automated):** By NAICS, award size and geography. Drop the giant primes; keep small and mid-size winners.
3. **Enrich (semi-automated):** Find the website, then the owner/ops lead, with an email/phone tool (Apollo, Hunter or similar, est. $50–$100/mo). Verify the entity is active (ClearMod's SOS/license lookups can be reused here; see section 4).
4. **Summarize (AI):** An LLM turns the award description into a plain-English scope plus a "why now" angle for the buyer's industry.
5. **Human QA (required):** Spot-check every row's contact against the company website. Remove anyone who is obviously not the decision-maker. Never ship unverified emails. A bad list kills trust fast.
6. **Deliver:** Shared Google Sheet plus a short email summary.

**Unit economics (est.)**
- Per buyer per month: ~3–5 hours (pull, QA, delivery). Most of the pipeline is shared: one Utah pull serves every buyer, so the marginal cost per extra buyer is roughly 1–2 hours.
- Costs: data/enrichment tools ~$100–$150/mo total, LLM API ~$10–$30/mo total, email-sending tool ~$40/mo.
- At 8 Feed buyers at $500 = $4,000/mo, with ~$250 in tools and ~30 hours/mo, that's roughly a **90%+ gross margin** (est.).
- The Meetings tier needs ~15–25 hrs/mo per client (est.). It's higher revenue but labor-heavy, so cap it at 2–3 clients.

**First 10 customers**
1. Build the sample list *before* pitching: 15 real recent Utah winners.
2. Build a target list: 60 Utah surety producers and commercial agents (state insurance department lookups, Google Maps, LinkedIn), plus 20 equipment rental and staffing firms.
3. Send cold email plus a cold-call follow-up within 48 hours (your strength). Offer a **$200 pilot month** to the first 5.
4. Ask your attorney contacts who their construction and insurance clients use for bonding; warm intros beat cold.
5. Attend one AGC of Utah or Associated Builders and Contractors event; surety producers are always there.

**Sample cold email**
> **Subject:** 14 Utah contractors who just won public work
>
> Hi {First},
>
> Every month, Utah contractors win federal, state and city contracts, and a lot of them need bonding or coverage fast. Most agents hear about it after the incumbent broker already did.
>
> I put together a list of 14 Utah companies that won public contracts in the last 30 days: award size, agency, scope, and the owner's direct contact. Here are 3 rows so you can judge the quality: {sample rows}.
>
> I'll send the full list, refreshed twice a month, for $200 for the first month. If none of them turn into a conversation, don't renew.
>
> Worth a look?
>
> Parker Jensen · {company} · {phone}

**Biggest risks and mitigations**
- **"I can get this myself."** Some agents already use free bid/award sites. *Mitigation:* the value is the verified *contact* plus the "why now" note. Lead with sample rows that are clearly better than a raw award record.
- **Email deliverability and compliance:** Follow CAN-SPAM (unsubscribe link, physical address). Use a separate sending domain. Warm it up.
- **Data accuracy:** Never claim an award you didn't verify against the source record. Keep the award ID in every row.
- **Churn:** Lists feel like a commodity after month 3. *Mitigation:* category exclusivity, and upsell to Meetings.

### #2: Competitor & Award Intel Reports (score 25)

**Exact offer:** A **"Bid Brief"** for a specific upcoming opportunity: incumbent(s), their past award values and modifications, likely competitors (who wins similar NAICS/agency work), pricing ranges from comparable awards, and a go/no-go recommendation. **5-business-day turnaround.** $500–$1,500 per brief, or a **$750/mo plan for 2 briefs plus a monthly "recompete radar"** listing contracts in the client's NAICS codes expiring in the next 6–18 months.

**Target customer:** Utah small businesses with 5–50 employees that already hold at least 1 federal contract (they understand the value). IT services, facilities, and construction near Hill AFB and other Utah federal sites. Utah defense contracting is large ($4.4B in 2019 per the [Gardner Policy Institute](https://gardner.utah.edu/defense-industry-contributes-19-3-billion-to-utah-economy)), but **76% of it goes to 10 contractors**, so the small-business pool is real but finite.

**Pricing evidence:** GovCon proposal/capture consultants in the DC market bill $100–$200/hr ([OST Global Solutions](https://www.ostglobalsolutions.com/how-much-do-consultants-charge)). A brief that takes a human analyst 8–12 hours is "worth" $800–$2,400 at those rates (est.); you deliver it in 2–3 hours with AI.

**Workflow:** USASpending award history plus FPDS-derived data for the incumbent and similar awards → AI drafts the narrative and pricing ranges → a human checks every number against the source records and writes the go/no-go. **The human check is non-negotiable:** a wrong incumbent value destroys credibility.

**Unit economics (est.):** 2–4 hrs per brief, ~$5 in API costs. At $750/mo per client for 2 briefs, ~6–8 hrs/mo per client, so **~$90–$125/hr effective**.

**First 10 customers:** Utah APEX Accelerator events and workshops (their clients are your buyers; APEX does free bid matching, but not paid competitive-intel deep dives, so you complement rather than compete; [Utah APEX](https://business.utah.gov/news/utah-ptac-becomes-apex-accelerator)). LinkedIn outreach to Utah small-business BD leads. Offer one free brief to 3 firms in exchange for a testimonial.

**Sample cold email**
> **Subject:** Who you're really bidding against on {solicitation #}
>
> Hi {First}, I saw {agency} has {opportunity} closing {date}. I pulled the award history: the incumbent has held it since {year}, with {N} modifications totaling {$}. I can send a 3-page brief on the incumbent, the 4 firms most likely to bid, and comparable pricing by {day}. First one's $250 (normally $750). Want it?

**Risks:** One-off by nature, so push the monthly plan. Software substitutes (HigherGov, SamSearch at $199–$415/mo; [SamSearch](https://samsearch.co/blog/top-10-govwin-alternatives-complete-2025-guide)) exist for DIY buyers, so only sell to people who want the *answer*, not the tool.

### #3: Workers' Comp Pre-Submission Packets (score 24)

**Exact offer:** An agent sends a prospect's name, state, class codes and payroll. **Within 1 business day** you return a PDF risk dossier: entity status, contractor license status, OSHA history, court/lawsuit hits, DOT safety (if vehicles), web/news red flags, missing-info checklist, and suggested market fit. **$50–$100 per packet, or $500–$900/mo for 10–15 packets.**

**Target customer:** Utah independent P&C agencies with 3–20 producers writing small-commercial workers' comp, especially those targeting contractors.

**Pricing evidence:** No direct market price exists for this exact deliverable, so all pricing here is an **estimate**. Anchors: a US-based CSR costs $45k–$55k base, $65k–$95k loaded ([HireInSouth](https://www.hireinsouth.com/post/insurance-virtual-assistant), [QuoteSweep](https://www.quotesweep.com/blog/hiring-vas-for-commercial-quoting)), roughly $35–$45/hr loaded (est.). If manual research takes 1.5–2 hrs per prospect (est.), a $75 packet saves them money. Offshore VAs at $8–$15/hr are the real competition ([Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/blog/virtual-assistant-for-insurance-agents)); your pitch is speed plus consistency plus a risk score, not cheap labor.

**Workflow:** Run ClearMod internally (you, not the client, operate it) → a human reviews every "UNCONFIRMED" field and fixes or notes it → PDF export → email to the agent.

**Unit economics (est.):** 20–30 min human time per packet; API plus search costs ~$1–$3 per packet. At $75/packet, that's **~$150/hr effective, ~95% gross margin**.

**First 10 customers:** Your workers' comp underwriting network. Utah Independent Insurance Agents association contacts. Offer **3 free packets** on their real open submissions; that's the fastest proof possible.

**Sample cold email**
> **Subject:** Pre-screen your next 3 WC submissions free
>
> Hi {First}, before you spend an hour working up a WC submission, you want to know if the insured has an inactive entity, an expired DOPL license, OSHA citations or lawsuits. I run that check and return a one-page risk packet within 1 business day. Send me your next 3 prospects and I'll do them free. If it saves you time, it's $75/packet after that.

**Risks and licensing:**
- **Insurance licensing:** Utah requires a producer license to *solicit, negotiate or sell* insurance ([Utah Code 31A-23a-103](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title31A/Chapter23A/C31A-23a_1800010118000101.pdf)). Delivering research to a licensed agent who does the selling *should* fall outside that. But **don't recommend specific carriers to the insured, don't quote premiums to insureds, and confirm with the Utah Insurance Department or an insurance attorney before launch.** Your attorney contacts can help.
- **Accuracy:** ClearMod's own "never upgrade unconfirmed data" rule is the right instinct. Keep it.
- **Small market:** This is a solid side line, not a $10k/mo business on its own unless you expand beyond Utah.

### #4: Teaming-Partner Matchmaking (score 23)

**Exact offer:** For a specific opportunity (or a standing NAICS focus), deliver within 5 business days a ranked shortlist of 10 potential primes or subs. Each entry includes why they fit (past awards, set-aside status, location), the contact, and a personalized intro email the client sends. **$750–$1,500/mo** for a standing service (2 opportunities/mo), or **$500 per one-off shortlist**. All pricing is an **estimate**; I found no published market rate for this exact service.

**Target customer:** Utah small businesses with set-aside status (8(a), WOSB, SDVOSB, HUBZone) seeking primes, and mid-size primes seeking small-business subs to meet subcontracting goals.

**Workflow:** USASpending subaward plus prime award data → filter by NAICS, agency and set-aside → AI writes the "why they fit" text and intro drafts → human verifies the contacts and checks that each company is active (SAM status, plus ClearMod's entity lookups).

**Unit economics (est.):** 4–6 hrs per shortlist; ~$300/mo in shared tools. That's **~$125–$175/hr effective** at $750 per 2 lists.

**First customers:** Utah APEX events, SBA Utah District Office events, LinkedIn. Pitch it as "we find you a prime" to small set-aside firms.

**Sample email (short):** "Hi {First}, {Prime} has won $X in {NAICS} at {agency} and has to hit small-business subcontracting goals. I pulled 10 primes who fit your certification and past performance, with intro drafts. Want the list? First one's $250."

**Risks:** The outcome (a signed teaming agreement) is slow and not in your control, so clients may churn before seeing value. Sell the shortlist as the deliverable, not the win. **Don't take a success fee tied to contract awards without legal advice:** federal contracting rules restrict contingent fees (historically the FAR "covenant against contingent fees," 52.203-5, with a "bona fide agency" exception; confirm the current rule). Your attorney contacts can tell you where the line is.

### #5: Done-for-You Bid Desk, SLED Focus (score 22)

**Exact offer:** **$2,500–$4,000/mo retainer** that includes weekly matched-bid digests plus **up to 2 drafted responses per month** for small state/local bids (janitorial, landscaping, IT, small construction). Or **$1,500–$3,000 per response** à la carte. **5–7 business days** for the draft; the client reviews, signs and submits.

**Target customer:** Utah service businesses with 5–50 employees that have *never* bid public work but could: janitorial, landscaping, snow removal, IT managed services, small GCs. They have the capacity but no one to do the paperwork.

**Pricing evidence:** Consultants bill $100–$200/hr in the DC market ([OST](https://www.ostglobalsolutions.com/how-much-do-consultants-charge)); freelance federal proposal roles run up to $45/hr ([OnlineJobs posting](https://www.onlinejobs.ph/jobseekers/job/1499161)). Win-bonus structures are used (e.g., a $200 bonus per award; [OnlineJobs posting](https://www.onlinejobs.ph/jobseekers/job/564384)). Grant-writing analogs: $1,500–$5,000 per foundation proposal, $2,000–$6,000/mo retainers ([Grantable](https://grantable.co/how-much-does-a-grant-writer-cost)), noting Grantable sells AI grant software and may skew high.

**Workflow:**
1. Bid matching: your platform plus state/county portals. Note that bid alerts alone are commodity: $29/mo to $500/yr ([CIRAS](https://newswire.ciras.iastate.edu/2025/09/12/ciras-bid-match-service-moves-to-paid-subscription-model), [ConstructionBids.ai on G2](https://www.g2.com/products/constructionbids-ai/discuss)). The money is in the *writing*.
2. AI extracts every requirement from the solicitation into a compliance matrix.
3. AI drafts each section from the client's own facts (an intake call, their past jobs, resumes, insurance certificates).
4. **Human writer edits everything**, and a second person checks every requirement against the matrix.
5. Client approves, signs, submits. **They submit, not you.**

**Unit economics (est.):** 10–20 hrs per small SLED response; ~$20 in API costs. At $3,000/mo for 2 responses, that's ~$75–$150/hr. It improves as you build a reusable library for each client.

**First customers:** Cold-call local service companies ("Have you ever bid city or school district work?"). APEX workshops. Offer the first response at cost.

**Sample cold email**
> **Subject:** {City} is buying {service} — due {date}
>
> Hi {First}, {City/School District} just posted a bid for {service}, due {date}. It's the kind of work you already do. Most companies your size skip these because the paperwork is a pain. We find the bids and draft the full response; you review and sign. Want me to send the solicitation and a 1-page plan for this one?

**Risks:** **Hallucinated qualifications.** Fabricated past performance or invented metrics can get a bid rejected or create legal exposure ([VisibleThread](https://www.visiblethread.com/blog/can-ai-be-trusted-in-government-contracting-proposals/), [Inventive](https://www.inventive.ai/blog-posts/ai-hallucination-in-rfp-responses)). *Mitigation:* every claim must trace to a client-provided document, and the client signs off on every factual statement. ### 1. Agenda Watch: local government meeting alerts (27)
- **Deliverable:** Every week, a short alert email when any Utah city council, county commission or planning commission agenda, minutes, or meeting recording mentions the client's topics: a rezoning near their land, a moratorium, a fee change, a competitor's project, or a vote on their application. Each alert includes a 3-line summary, a timestamped quote, and the link.
- **Who pays:** Real estate developers, land-use attorneys, commercial brokers, homebuilders, solar and cell-tower siting firms, and trade associations. **$300–$1,000/mo per client (est.)**, priced by number of jurisdictions.
- **Why AI fits:** Utah has hundreds of public bodies posting agendas (Utah's Public Notice Website centralizes many of them) and recordings that no human reads. Speech-to-text plus an LLM can read all of them each week. One pipeline serves every client.
- **Skeptic's note:** National startups already sell meeting-monitoring software. Your angle is a done-for-you analyst *service* with deep Utah small-town coverage. Before building, check how well existing tools cover Utah towns under 20k people.
- **License risk:** None. All of it is public information.

### 2. PDF accessibility remediation for public entities (23)
- **Deliverable:** You take a city's, school district's or special district's PDFs (agendas, budgets, forms) and return WCAG 2.1 AA–tagged versions, plus a monthly plan for new documents.
- **Who pays:** Utah cities, counties, school districts, special districts. Market rate is about **$5–$8/page**, with a $1–$11.50 range ([Equalify price compilation](https://equalify.uic.edu/EqualifyEverything/equalify-docs/blob/main/reflow/sources.md), [Skynet Technologies](https://www.skynettechnologies.com/blog/pdf-accessibility-cost-factors)). A monthly retainer could be $500–$2,000 (est.).
- **Why now:** DOJ's ADA Title II rule requires WCAG 2.1 AA. The deadline was pushed to **April 26, 2027 for entities with 50k+ population** and **April 26, 2028 for smaller ones and special districts**, and there's a pending lawsuit over the delay ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/doj-extends-digital-accessibility-compliance-dates-under-title-ii-of-the-ada/), [Accessible.org](https://accessible.org/news/doj-extends-ada-title-ii-web-compliance-deadline/)).
- **Skeptic's note:** Automation is pushing prices toward cents per page (Ohio State reported an automated approach far cheaper than manual; [ASU](https://smartchallenges.asu.edu/challenges/pdf-accessibility-ohio-state-university)). You win only on *done-for-you plus human-verified* quality. Automated checkers miss reading order and alt-text quality, so a human must check every document.
- **License risk:** None. The quality risk is that the client's compliance depends on your work, so put a clear scope in the contract.

### 3. Minutes-as-a-service for special districts and HOAs (23)
- **Deliverable:** They upload or send the meeting recording; you return draft minutes in their format within 48 hours.
- **Who pays:** Utah special service districts (water, sewer, fire, cemetery), small towns, HOA management companies. **$150–$400 per meeting (est.)**, monthly recurring.
- **Synergy:** This is the same transcript pipeline as Agenda Watch. The public body pays you to write its minutes, and those same meetings feed the alerts you sell to developers. Keep the two clearly separate so there's no conflict of interest: only use what is public once it's posted.
- **Skeptic's note:** Low price per unit, and some clerks will just use built-in AI meeting notes. Sell to bodies with no staff clerk.
- **License risk:** Low. Utah open-meetings law sets requirements for minutes, so the body's clerk must approve them; you deliver drafts only.

### 4. Utility and telecom bill recovery audits (22)
- **Deliverable:** You collect 24 months of a business's utility, telecom and waste bills; AI extracts every line item and flags wrong rate schedules, taxes, unused lines and duplicate charges. You file the refund requests with the provider.
- **Who pays:** Multi-location businesses: restaurant groups, churches, schools, property managers. **Contingency of 20–50% of recovered refunds and savings** is the industry norm ([Lightyear](https://lightyear.ai/blogs/telecom-expense-management-consultants)). One city contract paid 45% of refunds and 2 years of savings ([Urbana, IL ordinance](https://www.city.urbana.il.us/_Agendas_-_Packets_-_Minutes/Agendas_2006/06-05-2006/Ordinance_2006-05-062.pdf)).
- **Why AI fits:** Bill parsing and tariff comparison are exactly what LLMs do well.
- **Skeptic's note:** Cash is **slow**: you get paid only after refunds land, often months later. It's an old industry, so the edge is that you can serve *small* accounts that legacy auditors skip.
- **License risk:** Low. Some states regulate utility consultants; check Utah.

### 5. Preliminary-notice desk for subcontractors (21)
- **Deliverable:** Subcontractors and suppliers text you each new job. You file the Utah preliminary notice on the State Construction Registry within the deadline and track their lien and payment deadlines.
- **Who pays:** Small Utah subcontractors and material suppliers. **Per-notice fee or a monthly plan; $25–$50/notice or $150–$400/mo (est.)**.
- **Why it matters:** In Utah, the preliminary notice must be filed **within 20 days** of first furnishing labor or materials. Filing late loses lien rights for earlier work ([Levelset](https://www.levelset.com/blog/how-do-you-file-a-preliminary-notice-in-utah/), [Husch Blackwell](https://www.huschblackwell.com/utah-state-by-state-summary-of-construction-liens)). Confirm the deadlines in Utah Code 38-1a-501 and 502.
- **Skeptic's note:** Software (Levelset and others) already does this nationally. Small subs who hate software are your market.
- **License risk:** **Medium.** Filing preliminary notices is routinely done by non-lawyers, but drafting and recording *mechanic's liens* for others edges toward the practice of law. Stop at notices and deadline tracking, and refer liens to a partner attorney (your network).

### 6. GRAMA/FOIA records-request desk (21)
- **Deliverable:** A client tells you what they want ("all inspection reports on X," "emails about Y project"). You draft and file the request under Utah's GRAMA law or the federal FOIA, track it, follow up, appeal denials, and deliver a searchable, AI-summarized folder of the results.
- **Who pays:** Law firms (pre-litigation fact-gathering), private investigators, journalists, developers, consultants. **$150–$400 per request plus $200–$500/mo for tracking bundles (est.)**.
- **Fit:** Strong with your legal internship and government-data experience.
- **Skeptic's note:** Anyone *can* file a request themselves for free, so you sell persistence, tracking, and the summary. Response times are slow, so turnaround isn't in your control.
- **License risk:** Low for filing requests. Formal appeals to the State Records Committee or court go to the client's attorney.

### 7. Property tax appeal packets (20)
- **Deliverable:** For commercial and rental property owners, AI pulls comparable sales and assessed values, and you build the evidence packet for the county Board of Equalization appeal.
- **Who pays:** Owners of rentals, small commercial buildings, and multifamily. **Contingency of a share of first-year tax savings, or a flat $250–$750 per parcel (est.)**. I found no Utah fee data.
- **Timing problem:** The Utah appeal deadline is **September 15** (or 45 days after the county's notice, whichever is later) ([Utah Tax Commission decision](https://tax.utah.gov/commission/decision/24-2099.pdf), [Parsons Behle](https://parsonsbehle.com/insights/utah-property-tax-appeal-deadline-locally-assessed-commercial-industrial-multifamily-and-development-property)). The next window opens in summer 2027. **That's why Speed is 1:** it can't be your first business, but it's a good add-on next summer.
- **Skeptic's note:** Utah appeals require evidence such as comparable sales, which can be hard to get in a non-disclosure state like Utah. Test that comps are obtainable before committing.
- **License risk:** **Check** who may represent owners before the BOE and whether contingency fees are allowed. Entity owners need authorization paperwork.

### 8. Acquisition diligence packets for small-business buyers (20)
- **Deliverable:** For someone buying a $500k–$5M small business (often with an SBA loan), you assemble a diligence pack: business registration and litigation checks, liens and UCC filings, review and reputation analysis, customer concentration from the seller's data, and a list of questions to ask.
- **Who pays:** Search-fund buyers, first-time acquirers, small business brokers. **$1,500–$4,000 per deal (est.)**.
- **Fit:** Business finance plus pre-law, and ClearMod's entity, court and OSHA lookups reuse directly.
- **Skeptic's note:** Not recurring per client; recurring only through brokers who refer deals. Don't call it a "Quality of Earnings" report; that's CPA work.
- **License risk:** Medium. No legal opinions, no audited financials. The buyer's attorney and CPA sign off.

### 9. Settlement claims recovery for businesses (19)
- **Deliverable:** Scan new class-action settlements; find business clients who qualify; file their claims on contingency.
- **Who pays:** Businesses. Contingency fees are common, but I found no standard percentage.
- **Why it ranks low (blunt):** Courts are **hostile** to third-party filers. In the Visa/Mastercard interchange settlement, the court ordered filers to disclose that **merchants don't need to pay anyone** and that free help exists, and class counsel reports complaints about filers monthly ([court order](https://www.PaymentCardSettlement.com/Content/Documents/New%20Docs/Order%20Regarding%20Third-Party%20Claims%20Filing%20Services.pdf), [Digital Transactions](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/as-judge-mulls-settlement-a-thorny-issue-over-merchant-claims-roils-the-court/)). The reputation risk is high, and the work looks like taking a cut of free money. **Avoid** unless done with a law firm.

### 10. DOT driver-file compliance for small trucking fleets (18)
- **Deliverable:** You maintain each driver's qualification file (licenses, medical cards, annual reviews, MVR checks) and send renewal reminders before anything expires, so the fleet is audit-ready.
- **Who pays:** Utah fleets with 3–30 trucks, landscaping and construction companies with CDL drivers. **$15–$40/driver/month (est.)**.
- **Skeptic's note:** Established compliance companies sell this, and handling driver records means personal data. ClearMod's FMCSA lookup helps with monitoring.
- **License risk:** Medium, from privacy and data handling. A missed renewal is a real fine for the client, so get clear contract terms.
