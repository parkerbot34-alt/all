## 1. The market and our angle

- **Most small businesses already have a website (83% in a 2025 Clutch survey)**, but many are bad ([Clutch via Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250821848152/en/Clutch-Report-No-Code-Tools-Fuel-Website-Growth-Yet-17-of-Small-Businesses-are-Still-Offline)). Our main market is businesses with **bad** sites, not just ones with none.
- **Customers look businesses up before calling or visiting.** One roundup reports more than 60% of people check a business's Google profile before visiting ([WiserNotify](https://wisernotify.com/blog/google-business-profile-statistics/)). Benchmarks put calls at ~5–8% and website clicks at ~4–7% of Google profile views ([WebToNic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/google-business-profile-management-statistics)); these are vendor benchmarks, so treat them as rough. **That's why every package includes the Google profile, not just the website.**
- **What competitors charge:** budget freelancers $299–$499, mid-level $1,999–$2,999, typical freelancer range $1,500–$5,000; agencies $5,000–$15,000 ([Seahawk Media](https://seahawkmedia.com/tech/small-business-website-costs/), [WebsiteSetup](https://websitesetup.org/website-cost/)). Maintenance plans run $50–$500/mo ([Ueni citing GoDaddy](https://ueni.com/blog/website-maintenance-cost/)).
- **Our position:** Better than Fiverr, cheaper and faster than agencies, and local. We never compete on lowest price.

## 2. Who we serve (and how each type is different)

You said "really anything, mom-and-pops." That's fine for *who you'll accept*, but **outreach must focus on one type at a time** so your scripts, templates and examples fit. Start with Tier 1; add Tier 2 once you have 3 live sites.

| Tier | Business types | What they need most | Budget | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1: Start here** | Landscaping, plumbing, HVAC, house cleaning, roofing, handyman, auto detailing, junk removal, pest control | Calls and quote requests | Medium–high (one job = $300–$10,000+) | Easiest ROI pitch. Owners are in trucks and answer their phones |
| **Tier 2: Restaurants and food** | Family restaurants, cafés, bakeries, food trucks, taco shops | Menu, hours, location, photos, links to online ordering, Google profile | **Low–medium** | Tight budgets and turnover, *but* menus and hours change constantly, so care plans fit perfectly. Use their existing ordering system (see below) |
| **Tier 3: Other mom-and-pops** | Salons, barbers, auto repair, tailors, florists, daycares, music teachers, gyms | Bookings, hours, location, services, prices | Low–medium | Many already use a booking app (Square, Vagaro, etc.); we link to it |
| **Avoid for now** | Law, medical, dental, financial advisors, cannabis, e-commerce stores | | | Regulated advertising, privacy rules, or complex payments. Not worth the risk at the start |

### Restaurants: done right
- **Don't build custom online ordering.** Link to what they already use. Square offers a free branded ordering page on its free plan; Toast's ordering add-on is a monthly fee by quote ([Chowly](https://chowly.com/resources/blogs/toast-vs-square-for-restaurants/), [Sauce](https://www.getsauce.com/post/toast-vs-square-online-ordering)). Delivery apps typically take a 15–30% commission ([Otrucking guide](https://otrucking.com/resources/web-guides/restaurant-online-ordering-commission-free/)). **Pitch:** "Your website sends ordering customers to your own Square/Toast page so you skip the app commission." Verify what their system can do first.
- **The menu must be real text, not a PDF or photo.** It's readable on phones, Google can read it, and it's easy for us to update monthly.
- **Visit between 2 and 4 pm** (between lunch and dinner rushes), never during service.

## 4. How we build the websites (the production system)

We already have a working starter kit in `website-kit/` (template + build script + checklists). Here's the full system.

| 10. Handoff | Walkthrough video, care-plan signup, ask for a Google review and 2 referrals | Sales | 30 min |
| **Total** | | | **~9–15 hrs per Get Calls site; ~5–8 hrs per Get Found** |

### Monthly care work
- **1st of the month:** check every site loads, forms deliver, HTTPS valid.
- **Menu/hours/photo updates** within 2 business days of a request.
- **Google profile posts** per plan.
- **Monthly report:** visits, calls, form submissions, one improvement suggestion. Keep it short; the point is to show value so they keep paying.

### Quality rules (non-negotiable)
1. No made-up facts, reviews, ratings, licenses or awards. Everything is confirmed by the owner in writing.
2. Only the client's own photos or properly licensed images.
3. The client owns their domain, Google profile and email. We have access, not ownership.
4. Nothing launches without passing the QA checklist (`website-kit/README.md`, section 5).
5. Never promise Google rankings.

## 5. Team, roles and how we work together

### Roles (adjust to your actual people)

| Role | Owner | Responsibilities | Weekly hours (est.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sales Lead / CEO** | You | Prospecting, calls, walk-ins, demos, closing, proposals, invoicing, client relationships, weekly goals | 10–12 |
| **Builder / CTO** | Friend #1 (most technical) | Kit upgrades, building sites, launches, domains, technical fixes | 10 |
| **Content & Operations** | Friend #2 (most detail-oriented) | Intake, copy editing, Google profiles, QA, care-plan updates, monthly reports, pipeline tracker | 8–10 |

If you add a 4th person later, make it a second salesperson or a content/care-plan person. Sales is usually the bottleneck.

### How we work
- **Weekly 30-minute meeting** (same time every week): last week's numbers, pipeline review, who's stuck, next week's targets.
- **One shared group chat** for quick questions; **one tracker** (Google Sheet or Trello) as the source of truth.
- **Every client has one owner** responsible for its deadlines.
- **Class-schedule rule:** each person posts their week's availability by Sunday night. Client calls happen in the overlap windows.
- **Finals weeks:** no new launches scheduled during finals; tell clients timelines ahead of time.

### Ownership and money (decide at kickoff, put in writing)
- **Ownership split:** Options include equal thirds, or an equal base with a bonus pool for whoever closes deals. Equal thirds is simplest; give the closer a **10% commission off the top** on deals they close if sales effort is uneven.
- **Profit use:** e.g., **20% stays in the business** (tools, ads, taxes reserve), 80% split by ownership. Pay out monthly.
- **Vesting / leaving:** If someone quits in the first 6–12 months, what happens to their share and their clients? Decide now while everyone's friendly.
- **Decisions:** Majority vote for normal decisions; unanimous for adding owners, taking on debt, or selling the business.
- **Put it in an operating agreement** when you form the LLC. Ask a family attorney contact to review it; it's the most important document you'll sign together.

## 8. What happens with each client (the client journey)

1. **Prospect:** Found on Google Maps, logged in the tracker.
2. **Demo built:** Free sample homepage using their public info.
3. **Contacted:** Call, walk-in or text with the demo link.
4. **Conversation:** 10-minute call; show the demo, ask about their goals.
5. **Proposal:** 1-page proposal with the package, price, timeline and terms.
6. **Signed:** Contract signed, 50% deposit paid, intake form sent.
7. **Building:** Content gathered, site built (target: 7 days after intake is complete).
8. **QA:** Checklist passed.
9. **Review:** Client approves (2 revision rounds).
10. **Live:** Final payment, domain connected, Google profile set up.
11. **Care plan:** Monthly updates and reports.
12. **Referral:** Ask for a Google review and 2 referrals at launch, and again after their first good monthly report.

### Capacity check (important)
At 4 new sites a month plus 36 care clients, the work is roughly **40 hrs building + 36 hrs care + 40 hrs sales ≈ 116 hrs/month**, and the three of you have about **130 hrs/month** at 10 hrs/week each (est.). **You'll hit your time limit around months 8–10.** When that happens, raise prices (the best option), make templates faster, or add a 4th person.
