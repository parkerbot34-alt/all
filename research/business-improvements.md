## 1. Summary: the 5 things I'd do first

**The main point:** the website build is turning into a commodity. Wix launched an AI builder (Harmony) in January 2026, and its CEO wants Wix to power half of all new sites within 5–7 years. GoDaddy's Airo AI builder starts around $10/month. Meanwhile, buyers increasingly judge local businesses by **reviews and the Google profile**, not the website. **Treat the website as the way in, and make the money on monthly work that brings the owner customers he can see.** Today the care plans sell edits and hosting. They should sell **reviews, caught leads and an accurate Google profile.**

**The 3 biggest risks** (full list in section 3a):
1. **Commoditization plus AI search.** AI builders are making websites cheap. AI answers and summaries are cutting clicks (8% vs 15% of visits, per Pew via a secondary source). 45% of consumers used AI tools to find local businesses in 2026 (BrightLocal, secondary). *Defense:* sell reviews, lead response and profile accuracy, not pages.
2. **Crowded, legally risky outreach.** Scraped "no website" lists are sold to every agency. Owners hang up on Google-impersonation calls. The FCC applies do-not-call rules to texts, and courts have protected mixed-use cell phones. It's unclear whether Utah's Telephone Fraud Prevention Act (which can require a $25,000 bond) covers B2B calls. *Defense:* referrals, walk-ins, texting only with consent, and one hour with your family attorney.
3. **Churn plus liability on the recurring services.** About 1 in 5 new businesses close in their first year (BLS, via news). The FTC can seek up to $53,088 per fake-review violation. 3,117 federal website-accessibility lawsuits were filed in 2025, and food service is the #2 target. *Defense:* compliant review flows, accessible builds with no overlay widgets, ACH and annual billing, and tech professional-liability (E&O) insurance once revenue is steady.

## 3. Top 10: how to implement in the next 30–90 days

### #1 Reviews & Profile care plan (replaces "Standard")

**Why:** Reviews are what buyers read. BrightLocal's February 2026 survey (1,002 US adults) found 97% use reviews and 41% *always* read them, up from 29%. In secondary write-ups of the same survey, 31% only use businesses rated 4.5+ (up from 17%), and 74% look for reviews from the last 3 months. Google says ranking depends on relevance, distance and prominence, and that "more reviews and positive ratings can help." Google also says complete profiles make customers **2.7x** more likely to consider a business reputable and **70%** more likely to visit. An old BrightLocal number (2017) found 68% of consumers left a review when asked. **Asking works; most owners just don't ask.**

**What's in the plan (about $119/mo, est.):**
- **Review-request kit:** the official Google review link and QR code (generated in the desktop profile), a printed counter card, a line for invoices, and text/email templates the owner sends. Later, optional automated asks after each job (#2's tool).
- **Reply drafts for every review within 2 business days.** AI writes the draft, a teammate personalizes it, the owner approves. Templated replies turn off 50% of consumers, so no copy-paste.
- **Profile upkeep:** hours and holiday hours, services, photos, menu or booking links. Keep the business's name, address and phone matching across Google, Yelp, Facebook, Apple Maps and Bing (Google's share of review-site use fell to 71%, so the others matter). AI tools draw on the same sources.
- **Monthly proof report:** new reviews, average rating, reply rate, and profile calls, website clicks and direction requests.

**Compliance rules (write them into the procedures):**
- Ask **every** customer. Never filter who gets asked by sentiment. Google bans "selectively solicit[ing] positive reviews."
- No incentives, no staff quotas, no pressuring people in the shop (Google). Never write or buy reviews. The FTC can seek up to $53,088 per violation and sent warning letters in December 2025.
- Reviews shown on the website need the reviewer's permission (Google advises asking the author). Never imply the featured reviews are "all" the reviews (FTC rule).

**30–90 days:**
- **Days 1–14:** write the procedure and templates; set up the agency's own Google profile and review kit as the demo.
- **Days 15–45:** run it free for 30 days on the first 2–3 clients. Record baselines: review count, rating, days since the last review.
- **Days 46–90:** make it the default plan in proposals at $119 (est.). Grandfather existing $99 clients for 6 months.

**Metric:** new reviews per client per month, reply rate, plan uptake, churn.

### #2 Missed-call text-back + instant lead alerts

**Why:** In Invoca's 2026 home-services benchmark, a person answered 52% of calls (65% excluding calls of 15 seconds or less). Among calls from digital marketing, 38% were leads and 45% of those converted on the call. CallRail's January 2025 report (1.1M leads) put home services' missed-call rate at 14%. Invoca's data comes from companies big enough to buy enterprise call tracking, so treat both as rough. HBR (2011): firms that responded within an hour were nearly 7x more likely to qualify a lead than firms that waited one more hour, and 23% never responded. Google ended Business Profile chat in July 2024, so a website "text us" option plus text-back fills that gap. Since **Oct 1, 2026**, Google bills LSA advertisers for business-hours calls left unanswered for more than 20 seconds (Search Engine Roundtable / PPC Land, reporting Google's notice).

**Build or buy:**
- **Buy:** GoHighLevel at $97/mo (Starter; sources disagree on how many client accounts it allows), $297 (Unlimited) or $497 (resale tier). Usage (texts, calls) is billed on top.
- **Build:** a small Twilio app written with your AI coding tools. Cheaper per client, but you own the maintenance and compliance.
- **Rule:** **pre-sell to 2 home-service clients before paying for anything.**

**Compliance:**
- Each client's texting needs carrier (10DLC) registration. Twilio lists a $46 one-time brand fee (including vetting), a $15 campaign vetting fee, and $1.50–$10/mo per campaign. Charge a ~$99 setup fee (est.).
- Messages must be **replies only:** business name, a short line, "Reply STOP to opt out." No promotions.
- If an AI ever replies, say so at the start. That's the safe harbor in Utah's AI disclosure law (fines up to $2,500 per violation).

**Keeping the published number:** use the carrier's no-answer forwarding to the text-back number, so the number on Google and the website doesn't change (test with each carrier, est.). If a client wants a tracking number, follow Whitespark's method: tracking number as primary, real number as "additional."

**30–90 days:**
- **Days 1–30:** build or trial it on the agency's own line and test with every major carrier.
- **Days 31–60:** 2 pilot clients at a founding price.
- **Days 61–90:** put it in proposals as a $59/mo add-on and inside the $199 "Leads" plan (est.). Report missed calls caught and replies each month.

**Metric:** missed calls caught, reply rate, jobs the owner says came from it.

### #3 Referral-partner engine

**Why:** Owners pick vendors through people they trust. In The Alternative Board's survey, 46% most want other owners' opinion of a vendor, vs. 10% for the vendor's own claims. In OnPay's 2019 survey, 31% named their accountant as their most trusted advisor. Cold lists are crowded: Outscraper (500 free records/mo, then ~$3 per 1,000) and Apify tools sell "businesses without websites" to every agency.

**Who:**
- **CPAs and bookkeepers;** attorneys who form LLCs (your family network: they see new businesses first).
- **Insurance agents;** sign, vehicle-wrap and print shops; Square/Toast resellers; restaurant suppliers.
- **The UVU SBDC in Orem** (free one-on-one consulting for Utah and Wasatch counties, plus free monthly classes taught by local professionals). **SCORE.**
- **Chambers:** the Utah Valley Chamber, the Salt Lake Chamber (ribbon cuttings for new members), and the **Utah Hispanic Chamber**, which is reopening in Utah County at the Clyde Companies offices in Orem.

**The offer to partners:**
- $100 per signed client (already in the plan), or a free month of care for the partner's own business.
- A free "Google profile health check" they can offer their clients.
- A one-page explainer.
- Offer to **teach a free 45-minute "Get found on Google" class** at the SBDC or a chamber. Those classes are taught by community professionals.

**30–90 days:**
- **Days 1–30:** list 30 partners; Parker books 3–5 coffee meetings a week (it's sales work he's good at). Apply to the Jobber, Housecall Pro and Square partner programs (#11).
- **Days 31–60:** first class or workshop; partner one-pager; referral tracking column in the tracker.
- **Days 61–90:** drop partners who send nothing; give the top 5 a quarterly check-in.

**Metric:** active partners, introductions per month, close rate of referred vs. cold leads (expect referred to be higher, est.).

### #4 Trust & compliance package

**Why:** Owners have been burned. Podium sells on 12-month contracts. Hibu and Thryv reviewers describe trouble cancelling, auto-renewals and billing before delivery (individual reviews, not proof of a pattern). The FTC's Pointbreak case involved robocallers posing as Google who took $300–$700 to "verify" listings; refunds went to 4,467 owners. Google's help center warns that scammers claim to be "with Google."

**The package (put it in writing in every proposal):**
1. **Month-to-month care, no auto-renewing terms.** The only fixed term is the $0-down plan's 12 months, which then rolls to month-to-month.
2. **The client owns everything.** Domain, Google profile (client is primary owner, we're managers, per Google's guidance), analytics and site files. Cancel any time and keep the files (the plan's $100 move fee is fine).
3. **Live within 7 days of receiving content, or the first month of care is free.**
4. **Verifiable local identity:** real names, UVU affiliation, the LLC listed in the Utah registry, the agency's own Google reviews. Script line: *"We're not Google and never will claim to be."*
5. **Compliance built in:**
   - **Accessibility:** the kit already has alt text, labels, contrast and keyboard checks. Test against WCAG 2.1 AA, never install overlay widgets (the FTC fined accessiBe $1M over its overlay claims), and never promise "ADA compliance."
   - **Reviews:** compliant review flows (#1).
   - **Restaurants:** text menus, not PDFs. Food service was the #2 target of accessibility suits in UsableNet's mid-2025 report.

**Demo-site rules** (the demo-first pitch is your edge, so make it safe):
- Use only public facts: name, services, city, hours.
- **Don't copy their logo, photos or reviews.** Using a logo without permission risks a trademark claim (industry and forum opinion, not case law). Review text belongs to the reviewer, and Google advises asking the author first.
- Use placeholder images and a "Sample prepared for {Business}, not affiliated" banner.
- Set `noindex`, share only by link, and delete after 30 days with no reply.

**30–90 days:**
- **Week 1:** add the promises to the proposal and contract. Have the family attorney review them along with #13.
- **Week 2:** add a demo mode to the build (#7).

### #5 Billing and retention mechanics

**Why:** For B2B companies selling to small and mid-size businesses, 2.5–5% monthly churn is "good" and under 1.5% is "great" (ProfitWell data via Lenny's Newsletter). Failed payments cause roughly 20–40% of churn (Paddle via Chargeflow; Recurly's own page says 53%). ProfitWell data from ~2,500 companies says annual plans reduce churn "dramatically."

**What to set up:**
- **ACH autopay by default.**
  - Square Invoices: 1% per ACH payment ($1 minimum) on the free plan, with recurring autopay from a saved bank account.
  - Stripe: 0.8% per ACH debit, capped at $5. Stripe Billing adds ~0.7% (third-party figure).
  - Cards expire; bank accounts rarely do.
- **Annual prepay:** 12 months for the price of 10. Pitch it at launch, when the owner is happiest.
- **Keep-alive downgrade:** before any cancellation, offer the $49 plan (hosting, security, uptime; edits billed hourly).
- **90-day results review:** a 15-minute call with the proof report and one improvement idea. Then quarterly.
- **Proof report**, built once and automated (#7): new reviews, rating, reply rate, profile calls and clicks, form leads, missed calls caught.

**Metric:** monthly logo churn (target <3%, as in the plan), share on ACH, share on annual.

### #7 Productize and automate delivery

**Why:** The plan's own capacity check runs out of hours in months 8–10. Recurring services add hours unless they're standardized. These upgrades go in the kit's own backlog; I made no changes to `website-kit/`.

**What to standardize:**
1. **Templates per industry** (home services, restaurants with a text menu, salons and barbers with booking, auto). Multi-page support is already the kit's next item.
2. **Demo mode in `build.py`:** a `demo` flag that adds `noindex`, the "Sample" banner and placeholder images. **Target: a demo in 30 minutes or less** (est.), down from 1–2 hours.
3. **Intake:** Google Form → Sheet → a script that writes the client's JSON file.
4. **AI drafting with a human editor:** service pages, Google profile descriptions, review replies, the report summary. Keep the plan's rule: owner-confirmed facts only.
5. **Reports:**
   - Looker Studio's free Search Console connector covers search data.
   - Google profile numbers: copy them by hand at first. Google's Business Profile API requires an access request (reviewed within 14 days), so apply once you have 5+ clients.
6. **Procedures and time tracking:** a 1-page procedure for each recurring task (review replies, holiday hours, menu edits). Log minutes per client; **target ≤45 min per client per month** (est.).

**30–60 days:** the builder ships the demo mode and 2 industry templates first, then the intake script and report template.

## 4. Ideas considered and rejected

- **Pure pay-per-lead pricing:** disputes over which leads count, call-tracking costs (~$45–$50/mo per client) and cash-flow risk. Google LSA already sells leads (~$53 each in home services).
- **Standalone local SEO retainers:** the market charges $500–$1,000/mo and up (23% of SEOs, per Ahrefs' survey of 439 providers). You can't deliver it credibly part-time; keep the basics inside the care plans.
- **Google or Meta ads management (other than LSA setup):** needs client ad budgets, expertise and accountability for results. Not now.
- **Social media management:** eats hours, low margin, crowded, hard to prove value.
- **Weekly Google posts as a headline feature:** a small Sterling Sky test found no direct ranking effect. Keep posts for holiday hours and offers only.
- **Accessibility overlay widgets:** the FTC fined accessiBe $1M for overclaiming. Overlays create liability instead of removing it.
- **Review gating, incentives, review swaps, writing reviews for clients:** banned by Google, and the FTC can seek civil penalties.
- **Custom online ordering or e-commerce:** Square's free plan already covers pickup ordering, and payments plus inventory are a support trap.
- **Rank-and-rent / lead-gen sites with made-up listings:** against Google's profile eligibility rules, slow, and ethically murky.
- **Scraped mass outreach (autodialers, bulk texts, bulk cold email):** TCPA/do-not-call exposure, terms violations, and the same list every competitor has.
- **Competing on lowest price (Fiverr-style):** Wix and Airo will always be cheaper. Your edge is done-for-you, local and accountable.
- **Joining BNI right now:** ~$1,000–$2,000/yr all-in on a $0 budget. Visit as a guest, and join after revenue.
- **Call tracking on every client:** cost plus listing-consistency risk. Offer it only to clients who want proof.
- **Building your own software product now:** you don't have the customers or the time. Revisit in 3b.
- **Website chatbots for mom-and-pops:** low chat volume, maintenance work, Utah AI disclosure. Text-back covers the need.
- **Long or auto-renewing contracts:** they cut churn on paper but destroy your edge over Hibu and Thryv.
- **Registering domains or Google profiles in the agency's name to lock clients in:** against Google's guidance, and it will cost you trust and referrals.
- **AI receptionist resale right now:** wait until text-back shows owners will pay to fix call handling.
- **Free sites for "exposure" beyond 3 founding clients:** you get testimonials but not the cash you need.
