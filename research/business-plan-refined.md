13. **Founding prices:** now the first 6 clients, with no more than 2 per segment, so each segment gets a case study.
14. **Competitor prices re-sourced:** the "$299–$499 budget freelancer" figure credited to Seahawk could not be found. Utah-specific and DIY-builder prices replace it.
15. **Card fees corrected:** card invoices cost about 3.3% + 30¢, not "~3%." ACH is now the default for build fees.
16. **Restaurant pitch corrected:** a restaurant's own online ordering still has card fees (Square's free plan: 3.3% + 30¢ online). The 15–30% delivery commissions are now confirmed from DoorDash's own pricing.
17. **LLC moved earlier:** under Utah law, people who run a business together for profit are a general partnership by default, with personal liability. Form the LLC before taking the first deposit.
18. **City licensing:** now covers Orem, Provo, Salt Lake City and Lehi, plus Utah's limit on fees for home-based businesses.
19. **Sales tax:** added what Utah's own guidance says, and why a template-based build is a gray area for a CPA to answer.
20. **New legal flag, cold calls:** Utah's telemarketing law may require registration ($500 a year plus a bond) before cold calling. No cold calls until an attorney answers.
21. **New compliance rules:** CAN-SPAM for cold email, the FTC's fake-review rule, and Google's ban on review incentives and review gating.
22. **New workflows:** how leads, payments and handoffs move between teammates; a care-request log; and a policy for features we don't offer.
23. **New contract checklist** for the attorney to review.
24. **Kit described accurately:** single home page, home-services wording, no analytics (though its privacy page says it uses analytics), no demo mode. Fixes are prioritized.
25. **New demo rules:** demos are hidden from Google, labeled as samples, deleted after 30 days, and kept in one Cloudflare project to stay under its 100-project soft limit.
26. **New Day 1–14 and Day 15–60 plans,** built around UVU's fall break, Thanksgiving break and finals.
27. **Weak stats removed:** the WebToNic link (could not be found) and WiserNotify's "60%" (vendor blog, no original source). BrightLocal's survey replaces them, labeled.

## 1. The market

### Where
- **All of Utah.** Start in **Utah County and Salt Lake County**, where the team can walk in and meet owners cheaply. Sell to Davis/Weber, Cache and Washington counties by email, video call and (once cleared) phone after the pitch works locally.
- **Size is not the constraint.** Utah had **371,569 small businesses** (SBA Office of Advocacy 2025 profile, based on 2022 Census data) [official]. Many are one-person firms that aren't our target. Even so, the number of reachable mom-and-pops is far larger than a student team can serve. **Your hours are the constraint, not the market.**

### Who: no website, or a bad one
Nationally, **83% of small businesses have a website**, up from 64% in 2018, leaving **17% offline** (Clutch survey, August 2025) [third-party]. Of the offline ones, over a third said a site isn't relevant to them, and 28% had no online presence at all [third-party]. So most prospects will have *some* site. The pitch differs:

| Prospect has | What we say |
|---|---|
| **No website** | "People search for you and find a competitor. We'll get you a site and a complete Google profile in about 2 weeks." |
| **A bad website** | "Your site is losing calls. Here's a sample that works on a phone, loads fast and puts your number one tap away." |

**A site counts as "bad" if any of these are true** (check on a phone in 2 minutes):
- [ ] The website link on their Google profile is dead or goes nowhere. (Google shut down its free Business Profile websites in March 2024, and those links stopped redirecting in June 2024 [third-party]. Some profiles still point to them or to nothing.)
- [ ] Facebook page only.
- [ ] Hard to use on a phone (tiny text, sideways scrolling, broken layout).
- [ ] No tap-to-call number, or no contact or quote form.
- [ ] Restaurant menu is only a PDF or a photo, or it's out of date.
- [ ] Hours on the site don't match Google.
- [ ] No HTTPS ("Not secure" in the browser), broken images, or an old copyright year.

**Signals the business can pay:** 15+ Google reviews and a 4.0+ rating (est. heuristic; adjust after Day 30).

### Why the Google profile is in every package
- **71% of consumers use Google to read local business reviews**, down from 83% the year before (BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2026) [third-party].
- Vendor benchmarks put **calls at about 5–8% and website clicks at about 4–7% of Google profile views**, with service businesses often higher (WebFX; WiserNotify) [third-party]. Treat these as rough.
- Google counts "calls" as taps on the call button and "website clicks" as taps on the website link in the profile [official]. That's what we can report to clients each month.

## 2. Who we serve: three segments, all from week 1

Parker wants mom-and-pops of all kinds. The plan runs all three segments from the start. Each segment gets its own demo template, pitch and examples, because one generic pitch converts worse.

| Segment | Examples | What they need most | Main package | Kit ready? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Home services** | Plumbing, HVAC, cleaning, handyman, roofing, landscaping, detailing, junk removal, pest control | Calls and quote requests | Get Calls | **Yes** (current template is built for this) | High job value makes the payback pitch easy. Owners answer their phones |
| **Restaurants and food** | Family restaurants, cafés, bakeries, taco shops, food trucks | Real-text menu, hours, directions, photos, links to their own ordering | Menu & Map | **No.** Needs a menu template (Days 4–9) | Lower budgets. Menus change often, which fits a care plan |
| **Other local shops** | Salons, barbers, auto repair, tailors, florists, gyms, music teachers | Hours, services, prices, booking link, directions | Get Found or Get Calls | **Mostly.** Needs editable button and section labels (Day 4) | Many already use a booking app. We link to it |

### Recommended weekly mix (and why)
- **Days 1–8:** demos for home services and shops only, because the kit can already build them. Build the restaurant template at the same time. Restaurant demos start on Day 9.
- **Day 10 to Day 30:** split demos about **40% home services, 30% restaurants, 30% shops** (est.). Home services get the most because the payback math is easiest to show.
- **Day 30 review:** compare demos, conversations, proposals and signed clients by segment. Shift the mix toward what converts. Don't drop a segment until it has had about 25 demos.
- **Timing (reasoning, not data):** from now through December, heating, plumbing, cleaning and auto shops are in season. Landscapers are winding down, so pitch them in January–February for spring. Restaurants get busy around the holidays, so walk in mid-afternoon on weekdays and skip the week of Thanksgiving.

### Avoid for now
Law, medical, dental, financial advisors, cannabis, and full e-commerce stores. They bring regulated advertising, privacy rules or complex payments.

### Restaurants: done right
- **Don't build ordering. Link to what they have.** Square's free plan includes online ordering for pickup and local delivery at $0/month, with online card processing at **3.3% + 30¢** [official]. Toast markets commission-free online ordering; its plan pricing is by quote [official/third-party]. DoorDash also offers commission-free ordering through a restaurant's own website, at 2.9% + $0.30 per order [official].
- **The pitch, stated honestly:** DoorDash Marketplace charges **15% (Basic), 25% (Plus) or 30% (Premier)** on delivery orders and 6% on pickup [official]. "Your website sends customers to your own ordering page, where you pay card processing of about 3% instead of a 15–30% commission." Check what their system can do before you say it.
- **The menu must be real text,** not a PDF or photo. Phones can read it, Google can read it, and it's quick to update.
- **Visit between 2 and 4 pm** on weekdays, never during a rush.

## 4. How we build (the production system)

### 4.1 What the kit actually does today
Checked against `website-kit/build.py` and `website-kit/template/`:

**It does:**
- Builds one **home page**, a privacy page, a thank-you page, `robots.txt` and `sitemap.xml` from one JSON intake file. Python 3 only, no installs.
- Mobile layout with a sticky call bar, tap-to-call, a Web3Forms quote form with a spam trap and thank-you redirect.
- LocalBusiness structured data, page title, description, social tags.
- Escapes all client text. Shows a star rating only if real numbers are entered.

**It doesn't (yet):**
- **Multi-page sites.** Get Calls needs a page per service.
- **Restaurants.** No menu, no hours table, no order or reserve buttons.
- **Other shops.** Button and section text is hard-coded for home services ("Get a free quote," "Areas we serve," "Send my quote request").
- **Analytics.** There is no analytics code, yet the generated privacy page says "We also use privacy-friendly analytics." Add analytics or remove that sentence before the first launch.
- **A demo mode.** Every build allows search engines to index it. Demos need `noindex`, a "sample" banner and a disabled form.
- **A leftover-placeholder check.** The README says no `EXAMPLE` or `PLACEHOLDER` text may remain, but the script doesn't check.
- Image compression (use Squoosh by hand for now), color or layout variants, Spanish.

### 4.4 Demo rules
- Build demos only from public info: business name, phone, hours, services, and up to 3 public reviews.

- Never post a demo publicly or in the portfolio unless the business becomes a client and agrees.
- Ask the attorney whether a private demo using a business's name and public reviews raises any issue (section 10.3).

### 4.6 Quality rules (non-negotiable)
1. No made-up facts, reviews, ratings, licenses or awards. The owner confirms every fact in writing.
2. Only the client's own photos or properly licensed images.
3. The client owns their domain, Google profile and form email. We get access, not ownership.
4. Nothing launches without passing the QA checklist (`website-kit/README.md`, section 5), checked by someone other than the builder.
5. Never promise Google rankings or a number of leads.
6. **Reviews:** the FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule took effect October 21, 2024. It bans fake reviews, buying reviews and suppressing negative ones, with civil penalties that FTC warning letters in December 2025 put at up to $53,088 per violation [official]. Google bans offering anything in exchange for reviews and bans asking only happy customers ("review gating") [official]. **Any review-request page or QR code we build must go to every customer, with no filter.**

### 5.2 Handoffs (what "done" means)
- **Sales → Content (after Won):** signed contract and deposit receipt in the client folder; intake form sent; intake call booked.
- **Content → Builder:** intake JSON filled, photos compressed in the client's asset folder, every fact marked "owner confirmed," Web3Forms key and domain access in the password manager.
- **Builder → QA:** preview link, list of anything not done, build passes the placeholder check.
- **QA → Sales:** checklist passed, or a fix list goes back to the Builder.
- **Sales → Builder (launch):** written approval **and** final payment received.
- **Builder → Content & Ops (care):** site live, analytics on, monitoring on, care plan and renewal date in the tracker.

### 5.3 How money flows
1. **No invoices until the LLC, EIN and business bank account exist** (target Day 12). If a client says yes earlier, schedule the invoice for when the account is ready.
2. All invoices go out from **one** Stripe or Square account in the LLC's name, paying into the business bank account. Never into a personal account.
3. Sales sends the deposit invoice. Content & Ops logs each payment in the Money tab the same day.
4. Builder does not launch until the Money tab shows the final payment.
5. Care plans: autopay set up at handoff. A failed payment gets a reminder within 2 days. After 14 days unpaid, care work pauses (put this in the contract).
6. Monthly, by the 5th: Content & Ops totals revenue and costs; the team pays any commissions, keeps the reserve and splits the rest as the written agreement says.

### 5.4 Tracking care-plan work
- **One way in:** clients send requests through a care-request Google Form or the agency email. A request that arrives by text gets logged by whoever received it the same day.
- **Care log** (tracker tab), one row per request: date, client, plan, request, estimated minutes, assigned to, done date, minutes spent, inside plan or overage, client approved overage (Y/N).
- **Monthly:** total minutes per client against the plan allowance. Bill approved overage on the next invoice.
- **1st of each month:** check every site loads, every form delivers, HTTPS is valid. Log the result.
- **By the 5th:** send each Standard and Plus client a 1-page report: Google profile views, calls, website clicks and direction requests (from the profile's Performance page; it needs a verified profile, and new data can take up to 5 days [official]), website visits, form submissions, what we changed, and one suggestion.

### 5.5 When a client wants something we don't offer
1. Sales says: "Let me check with the team. I'll get back to you within 2 business days." **Never promise in the room.**
2. Builder estimates the hours and says whether it fits a static site.
3. **Link, don't build:**

| Request | Answer |
|---|---|
| Online ordering | Link to their Square, Toast or DoorDash ordering page |
| Online booking | Link to their booking tool (or set one up on the client's account) |
| Selling products online | Refer to Square Online or Shopify; link from the site |
| Blog, logins, memberships, custom apps, ongoing ads or SEO campaigns | Decline for now and refer out |
| Small extras (an extra section, a simple FAQ, a new page) | Quote at the extra-page price or $50/hr |

4. If it's under about 2 hours and fits the kit, quote it. If not, decline politely and refer. Keep a short list of people to refer to (a freelance developer, a photographer, a print shop).

### 5.6 Tracker layout (one Google Sheet)
- **Pipeline:** business, segment, city, phone, owner name, site status (none / bad + reason), reviews and rating, demo link, stage, client owner, next action and date, number of touches, channel, package, price, deposit date, final payment date, launch date, care plan, care start, lost reason, referral source.
- **Care log:** as in 5.4.
- **Money:** date, client, invoice, amount, method (ACH or card), fee, commission owed.
- **Time log:** person, date, client or "demo" or "admin," hours. Two minutes a day. It's how you learn your real hours per client, and it supports an hours-based profit split if you choose one.

### 6.3 How we work
- **Weekly 30-minute meeting** on Sunday evening: last week's numbers, pipeline, who's stuck, next week's targets. Everyone posts the coming week's availability before it.
- **One group chat** for quick questions. **The tracker is the source of truth.** If it isn't in the tracker, it didn't happen.
- **One client owner per client.**
- **QA is never done by the builder of that site.**
- **No launches during finals weeks.** Tell clients the timeline before they sign.

### 6.4 Ownership and money (decide at kickoff, put in writing)
- **Option A, equal split:** equal ownership; the closer gets 10% of the build fee on deals they close; profit split equally.
- **Option B, hours-weighted (better for uneven part-time hours):** equal ownership, but monthly payouts split by hours in the time log.
- **Either way:**
  - Keep **20% of profit** in the business as a reserve (tools, a cushion, and money for taxes the business may owe).
  - Each person also sets aside about **25% of what they receive** for their own taxes until a CPA gives a better number (section 10).
  - **Leaving:** decide now what happens to a person's share and clients if they quit in the first 6–12 months. One option is ownership that is earned over 12 months, with nothing earned in the first 3 (est. suggestion; attorney to draft).
  - **Code and work belong to the LLC.** Everyone assigns what they build to the LLC, including the kit and templates.
  - **Decisions:** majority for normal decisions; unanimous for adding owners, taking on debt or selling.
- Put it in the **operating agreement** when you form the LLC. Have a family attorney contact review it.

### 10.4 Questions for a CPA

### 10.5 Questions for an attorney
1. **Before any cold calls:** does Utah's Telephone Fraud Prevention Act (as amended by SB 38, 2026) or Utah's Telephone and Facsimile Solicitation Act require registration or a bond for calling businesses? How do federal Do Not Call and TCPA rules apply to owners' personal cell phones?

3. Draft or review the operating agreement: vesting, a member leaving, assigning code and templates to the LLC, deadlocks.

5. Is a private demo that uses a business's name, hours and public reviews a problem?
6. Review the privacy policy template. Should sites carry an accessibility statement?

8. Does Cloudflare's free plan allow hosting paid client sites?

## 14. Risks and how we handle them

| Risk | How we handle it |
|---|---|
| **Demos eat all our time** | Track demos per client; share demo building; test "ask first"; delete stale demos |
| **Nobody buys** | Stop-and-rethink rules (section 12). Change segment, pitch or offer; don't just repeat |
| **Cold calls need registration** | No cold calls until the attorney answers. Walk-ins and email first |
| **Personal liability before the LLC** | Form the LLC by Day 8; no deposits before the business bank account |
| **Sales tax surprise** | CPA question before the first invoice; separate line items; "plus applicable tax" in contracts |
| **A teammate stops showing up** | Written agreement on leaving; one owner per client; the tracker shows who has what |
| **School gets in the way** | Sunday availability posts; no launches in finals weeks; capacity planned at 35 hours, not 43 |
| **Scope creep** | Written scope; 2 rounds; section 5.5 policy; $50/hr |
| **Late or missed payments** | 50% upfront; launch after final payment; autopay; care pauses after 14 days unpaid |
| **$0-down client stops paying** | Autopay; early-cancellation clause; cap at about 1 in 3 clients |
| **Low-quality AI copy** | Human edits every page; owner-confirmed facts only; QA by a non-builder |
| **Fake-review or review-gating trouble** | Quality rule 6 |
| **Restaurants close or churn** | Collect fees upfront; watch restaurant share of care revenue |
| **Free tool limits or terms change** | Static sites backed up in GitHub; one project for all demos (Cloudflare's 100-project soft limit); re-check terms yearly |
| **Running out of hours** | Raise prices first, then speed up demos, then add the person who fixes the bottleneck |
